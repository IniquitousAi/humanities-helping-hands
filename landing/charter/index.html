The Charter

The rules we keep so you can hold us to them.The rules this library keeps, written down first, so anyone can hold us to them.The binding doctrines of Humanities Helping Hands, published first, machine-checked where formalized, enforceable by any reader.

Front page · The Founders · The Human Autobiography · Open Problems

"We compound what we know, or we eventually go extinct." (Daniel Steadham, founder)

REASONED
If we keep what we learn, we grow. If we forget it, we start over. Not checked by a machine.
A species that keeps losing what it learned falls behind over a long enough time; one that builds on it has a shot. History backs it: writing, the printing press, science. Not proven by a machine.
A species that preserves and compounds verified knowledge accumulates capability super-linearly; one that loses it resets to zero. Sourced in historical transitions (writing, printing, formalization). Not proven by a machine.
  1. Only a computer check earns PROVEN. No vote and no money can stamp a fact. A computer decides.
  2. Every fact shows its receipt. Each fact says how it was checked, so you can re-run it yourself.
  3. Five labels of truth, never mixed. PROVEN (a computer checked it), MEASURED (counted for real), CHECKED (a trusted source says the same), REASONED (makes sense by logic), GUESS (not checked yet). Three more tags: OPEN (unsolved), BLOCKED (this road failed) and ROOT (a starting rule, assumed, never proven).
  4. Every fact says what is not checked. A proof covers exactly what it states and nothing more.
  5. Money never changes a label. Paying for faster checks buys speed, never a verdict.
  6. Fix it at the root. Fixing 1 mistake where it starts beats fixing every copy. (The Root Theorem).
  7. People steer. The library answers the questions people bring. It never tells them what to want.
  8. Yours stays yours. Checking runs in your browser. What you type never leaves your device.
  9. Credit goes to proven work only. Contributors are named for what they proved.

  1. Only a machine check earns PROVEN. No vote, no expert and no amount of money can stamp a fact. A checker that cannot be talked into anything decides.
  2. Every label shows its receipt. Each fact says how it was checked, and anyone can run the check again themselves.
  3. Five labels of truth, never blurred. PROVEN (a machine checked it), MEASURED (read from the real thing), CHECKED (a trusted source says the same, its words shown), REASONED (follows by logic), GUESS (not checked yet). We never sell a guess as more than it is. Three more tags say where a piece stands, never how true it is: OPEN (nobody has settled it), BLOCKED (a published theorem shows this road cannot finish alone) and ROOT (a starting rule, assumed, never proven).
  4. Every fact says what is not proven. A proof covers exactly what it states and nothing more, and the card says where it stops.
  5. Money never changes a label. Paying for faster checks buys speed, never a verdict. The door flatters nobody, the founder included.
  6. Fix it at the root. A mistake is fixed once, where it starts, not again in every layer built on top of it. (Daniel's Root Theorem. In Lean it proves the model: if n ≥ 1 then c ≤ n×c. That this matches real mistakes is the model's reading, labeled REASONED.)
  7. People steer. The library answers the questions people bring. It never decides for them what to want.
  8. Yours stays yours. Checking runs in your own browser. What you paste and your own AI key never come to us.
  9. Credit goes to proven work only. Contributors are named for what they proved, and lose that credit if their work later fails a check.
  10. Nothing is called built until it is tested. A test counts only if it catches mistakes planted on purpose.
  1. Only a machine check earns PROVEN. No vote, no authority and no funding level can validate a theorem. Formal verification by a deterministic kernel decides.
  2. Every label shows its receipt. Each fact exposes its verification artifact (Lean AST with SHA-256 digest), fully reproducible locally.
  3. Five epistemic tiers, strictly partitioned. PROVEN (machine-verified proof/computation), MEASURED (empirical observations with sample bounds), CHECKED (agreement with a cited authoritative source, quoted), REASONED (first-order deduction from stated premises), GUESS (unverified hypothesis). Status tags: OPEN (undecided problem), BLOCKED (no-go theorem or barrier established) and ROOT (axiomatic foundation, assumed without proof).
  4. Formal scope boundary declared. Every proof specifies its domain, preconditions, and non-claims; cards explicitly bound validity.
  5. Financial decoupling from verification. Paid verification tiers allocate computing priority, strictly invariant of theorem acceptance or rejection.
  6. Root remediation invariant. Remediation at depth 0 strictly dominates leaf remediation once errors propagate across 1 or more dependent layers (Daniel's Root Theorem: n ≥ 1 → c ≤ n×c).
  7. User sovereignty in query selection. The library formalizes queries submitted by users, without prescriptive prioritization of truth value.
  8. Local execution and zero-knowledge privacy. Verification occurs client-side within browser sandbox; zero telemetry, zero input retention, zero credential exfiltration.
  9. Attribution strictly conditioned on verification. Contributor provenance attaches upon CI passage and revokes upon reproducible falsification.
  10. Falsifiable verification via negative mutation. Test harnesses are valid only if they detect planted negative mutants (fault injection).
