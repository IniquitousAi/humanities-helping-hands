Open problems · P vs NP

A million-dollar question, cut into small steps.

Checking an answer is fast. Is finding one always slower? Nobody knows; it has been open since 1971. We log published attempts, and where each one broke once someone traces it, so nobody has to redo it.

Checking is fast.

Guess

Is the gap real?

Most experts guess yes. Nobody has proven it. A correct proof wins one million dollars.

Ours

Checking an answer is fast, and right. For any SAT puzzle (a list of rules, each needing one of its switches set right) and any proposed setting of the switches, our checker says yes exactly when every rule is met. It takes at most one step per switch mention and per rule, plus one. This is the "checking is easy" half of NP.

Checked by Lean, a proof checker, on our computer: theorem PvsNP.sat_check_is_fast_and_right, no gaps, resting only on Lean's standard axioms. A known fact, now machine-checked. A planted false version (one step too few) was refused.
Proven
1975

Proving them different by simulation tricks alone. The trick that proves some problems take longer than others (diagonalization) cannot work here: Baker, Gill and Solovay built imaginary worlds where P equals NP and others where it does not, and these tricks cannot tell the worlds apart.

Paper: doi 10.1137/0204037 (SIAM Journal on Computing, 1975).
Closed road
1993

Proving it with "natural" circuit arguments. Razborov and Rudich showed the usual counting style of argument would also break the codes that keep the internet safe, so if those codes are safe, that style cannot finish the job.

Paper: doi 10.1006/jcss.1997.1494 (the journal version, Journal of Computer and System Sciences, 1997).
Closed road
2008

Proving it by turning logic into algebra alone. Aaronson and Wigderson showed the algebra trick behind famous results like IP = PSPACE also cannot decide P vs NP by itself.

Paper: doi 10.1145/1490270.1490272 (the journal version, ACM Transactions on Computation Theory, 2009).
Closed road
2016

The simplest geometric route. Not one of the three general barriers above: a block on one road only. Geometric Complexity Theory hoped for simple "obstructions"; Burgisser, Ikenmeyer and Panova proved the simplest kind does not exist. Richer kinds are still open.

Paper: doi 10.1090/jams/908 (the journal version, Journal of the American Mathematical Society, volume 32, 2019; online 2018).

A post on X said a lab had solved it in secret. It spread fast. Here is each piece of the story with its label and its source, opened on Nov 26, 2026.

P vs NP is still unsolved. The institute that offers the million-dollar prize lists it as unsolved on its own pages.

Source: the Clay Mathematics Institute's P vs NP page (www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/) and its list of the seven problems (www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/).

Proven
Codes

Even a real proof would not hand anyone a code breaker. The new results do not prove P = NP. Factoring is in NP but is not known to be among the hardest NP problems. And a proof of P = NP that is non-constructive, or whose method is far too slow in practice, would not break codes in real time.
