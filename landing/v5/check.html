The checker

Paste an answer, get the labels.

Each sentence is checked on its own. Sums, fractions, decimals, percents, units, primes and the facts in the Atlas come back with one of the four labels and a receipt anyone can rerun: Proven, true or false, when the machine works it out. Anything else is a Guess, not checked yet, and says why, which never means false.

Your words

What checked it

What it can check today: sums and comparisons with exact fractions (so 0.1 + 0.2 = 0.3 holds), percents, unit conversions, rounding, primes, number words, and the facts in the Atlas, word for word. History, health, news and opinions are settled by sources, not math: they are a Guess, not checked yet.

The four labels

What each answer means.

The label says how a claim was checked. Beside a Proven one, a plain word says whether it holds: true or false.

Proven

Only for a claim a machine checked. True: it holds. False: it does not.

The receipt names the exact math it ran, and a false one says what it really comes to.
Measured

Tested or measured, and the page says which test.

No check here gives it yet: no test of the world runs in this box.
Reasoned

Backed by sources or studies, with how strong the evidence is.

An Atlas fact whose numbers a machine checked, while its real-world part rests on its source.
Guess

Not checked yet. It says which checker is missing.

A guess never means false.
