Settled

One piece checked by machine, four roads closed by theorem.

Lean

Checking a SAT answer is fast and right. For any CNF formula and any assignment, the checker accepts exactly when every clause is satisfied, in at most one step per literal occurrence and per clause, plus one: the verification half of NP.

Theorem PvsNP.sat_check_is_fast_and_right, checked by Lean with no sorry, standard axioms only. A planted false bound (one step too few) was refused.
Proven
1975

Relativization. Baker, Gill and Solovay: there are oracles A and B with P^A = NP^A and P^B ≠ NP^B, so no relativizing argument (pure diagonalization or simulation) settles P vs NP.

doi 10.1137/0204037 (SIAM Journal on Computing, 1975).
Closed road
1993

Natural proofs. Razborov and Rudich: a natural, constructive and large, circuit lower bound argument against P/poly would break pseudorandom functions, so if strong one-way functions exist, natural proofs cannot separate P from NP.

doi 10.1006/jcss.1997.1494 (journal version, Journal of Computer and System Sciences, 1997).
Closed road
2008

Algebrization. Aaronson and Wigderson: the arithmetization techniques behind IP = PSPACE algebrize, and algebrizing techniques cannot resolve P vs NP.

doi 10.1145/1490270.1490272 (journal version, ACM Transactions on Computation Theory, 2009).
Closed road
2016

Occurrence obstructions in GCT. Burgisser, Ikenmeyer and Panova: occurrence obstructions cannot separate the permanent from the determinant, so the simplest obstructions Geometric Complexity Theory hoped for do not exist; multiplicity obstructions remain open. A block on one program, not a general barrier.

doi 10.1090/jams/908 (journal version, Journal of the American Mathematical Society, volume 32, 2019; online 2018).
Closed road
