What we collect

Nothing personal from reading the site. Most checks run on your own device. One page, Check this yourself, sends your sentence to us so a machine can check it. If the machine can check it, the checked math or fact goes on a public list, with no name on it.

If you add a question to the Open Problems list: the words you type are saved, and everyone can see them once two visitors asked the same thing on the same day. Do not put your name in it.

If you join the newsletter: just your email address, to send you news.

If you join the Founders Circle: your GitHub name, strength, and weakness. We only show it if you tick the box. To stop people dodging the 3-a-day limit, a code is kept with your application. The key that made the code is deleted after 2 days, and then the code cannot be matched to anyone.

If an AI asks our helper: it answers once and forgets it. Nothing you ask is saved.

On your own device: your browser remembers your reading level, your last check, your page in the Book and a Founders form you have not sent yet, so they are there when you come back. They stay on your device. We never get them. The Clear button wipes your last check.

Nothing personal from reading the site. Most checking runs in your own browser, and what you type there stays on your device. One page is different: Check this yourself (/check/yours/), like the check page of the older version 2, sends your sentence to our server so a machine can check it. When the machine can check it, one row is added to the public check log: the checked claim (the arithmetic, or the library fact it matched, never your own wording), the result and the time. No name, no address (IP) and no browser name. A one-way code made from your connection and a key that changes daily counts your logged checks, 20 a day at most, and is deleted after 2 days.

If a search finds nothing and you add your question to the public Open Problems list: the words you typed, how many times it was asked, and the first and last day. It shows publicly once 2 different visitors asked it on the same day, with any email, phone number or link taken out of what is shown. A one-way code made from your connection and a key that changes daily limits you to 10 adds a day and is deleted after 2 days. Each question also keeps a 12 character mark for up to 50 askers (the day and 5 characters of that day's code); they match nothing once that day's key is deleted, 2 days later.

If you join the newsletter: your email address, nothing else.

If you join the Founders Circle (the form on the Founders page): your strength, weakness, why you want to help, a link to something you built, your GitHub name, the trial task you were given and the day. No email and no address (IP). Only if you tick the box are your GitHub name, strength and weakness shown in the public strength finder, with any email, phone number or link in them removed first. To stop the 3-a-day limit being dodged, a one-way code made from your connection and a key that changes daily is kept with your application. The key is deleted after 2 days, and after that the code cannot be matched to a connection.

If your AI app uses the HHH connector (the MCP server at /mcp): the search words or sentence it asks about, used once to answer and then dropped. Your address (IP) is read only to keep a short in-memory count for rate limiting; it is never saved or put in an answer.

Kept on your own device, where we never get them: your reading level, your light or dark choice and the way you like results shown. Also your last check on the check page (until you press Clear), your page in the Book, and the answers in a Founders form you have not sent yet (removed once it is sent). They sit in your browser's own storage for this site; clearing the site's data in your browser removes them. A link you copy to share a check carries the claim in the last part of the address, after its hash mark, and a browser never sends that part to a server.

Static pages and the in-browser checker send no payload. POST /api/check (used by /check/yours/ and the version 2 check page) sends the claim to a Pages Function; a machine-checked result appends one row to the public check log: normalized claim, label, check id, hashes, run id and timestamp. No IP, user agent or free text is stored. Logged rows are capped at 20 a day per device, keyed by SHA-256(daily_salt | client network) with a 2-day TTL.

POST /api/asked persists the question text (200 characters at most), its count, first and last day, and up to 50 marks of 12 characters (the day plus 5 hex of that day's device hash). A row is public once it has 2 distinct marks from the same day, and a row whose key is on the hidden list is never public; display strips emails, phone numbers and URIs. Adds are capped at 10 a day per device, keyed by SHA-256(daily_salt | client network) with a 2-day TTL.

Newsletter subscriptions collect email only, transmitted to Buttondown via API over TLS.

Founders Circle applications persist: applicant GitHub handle, strength, weakness, motivation text, portfolio URI, trial task, the day, and that day's device hash. Text is stored as written. Public directory display requires explicit consent (optin=true) and strips emails, phone numbers and URIs from the displayed fields. The 3-a-day submission ceiling uses a counter keyed by SHA-256(daily_salt | client network) in Cloudflare KV with a 2-day TTL; the same hash is stored with the application and is unlinkable once its salt expires, 2 days later.

MCP endpoints at /mcp evaluate stateless JSON-RPC calls over HTTP; IP addresses are held in ephemeral memory for the 60 calls/minute bucket and never logged.

Client-side state only: localStorage holds the reading level (hhh-level), the theme (hhh-theme), the results view (hhh-view), the last checked text (hhh-last-check, removed by Clear), the Book page (hhh-book-page) and an unsent Founders form (hhh-fc-draft, removed on a successful send). None of it is transmitted. One more name ships with the newest version and is not in use: hhh-fc-answer. That version's Founders page would write it after a successful send, but visitors cannot open that page today, because its address forwards to the scoreboard. The record would hold the trial task (kind, title, body), its GitHub issue link, the typed answer and a timestamp, rewritten on each keystroke in the answer box. It would be read back for 24 hours after the last write and deleted on the next load of that page after that. It would never be posted to this origin; the answer would leave the browser only in the query string of the GitHub link, when the visitor presses Open it on GitHub. A shared check link and an Atlas search link carry their state in the URL fragment (q, find), which user agents do not put in a request.

Why we use it

Your email: only to send the newsletter. Computer helper: only to answer your question.

Your email: only to send the newsletter. Connector requests: only to answer that one request. The rate-limit count: only to stop the connector being overloaded.

Email identifiers: routing of transactional and notification dispatches. Ephemeral RPC: payload computation. In-memory sliding buckets: DDoS mitigation and API quota preservation.

Who else sees it

No ads, no data selling, no tracking cookies. Cloudflare hosts the site. Buttondown sends the emails. If you pay, Stripe takes the payment on its own page, and we never see your card number. Lookups happen only when you click one, and then Wikipedia gets that one sentence. We do not track you.

The Founders form uses Cloudflare Turnstile to check you are a person. The scoreboard shows each person's public X picture, which we get from unavatar.io. If you pay for a stamp, we keep your file for 90 days so it can be checked, then it is gone.

Cloudflare hosts the site and the connector, and may keep its own standard security logs under its own policy. Buttondown holds newsletter addresses and sends the emails.

Stripe takes payments on its own page, under its own policy, and your card number never reaches this site. Today the pay buttons are in test mode and no real money moves. After a payment we keep Stripe's order numbers, the fingerprint of your file, the amount and the time. No name and no email is kept with a payment on this site; Stripe holds those. If you email your file to hello@humanitieshelpinghands.com, that email reaches our inbox like any other email.

Lookups happen only when you click one. Wikipedia and Wikidata get that one sentence from your browser. Grokipedia lookups go through a small relay on this site that asks for one article title and may cache the article for a day. The title is not saved with anything about you. A one-way code made from your connection and a key that changes daily counts lookups, 60 a day per device, and is deleted after 2 days.

No ads, no selling data and no tracking cookies. Our own visitor counter counts each device once a day. It keeps only a one-way code made from your address (for an IPv6 connection, its network part) mixed with a random key that changes every day. It deletes that code after 2 days, never keeps the address itself, and skips bots. Cloudflare Web Analytics, which is cookieless, may also count. It counts visits and page views so the front page can show them, and it does not set a cookie or follow you to other sites. We never keep your address (IP) itself, and we guess only how many visitors block the counter, never who. Browsers that block it are simply not counted.

The Founders form uses Cloudflare Turnstile, under Cloudflare's own policy, to tell a person from a bot before it takes an application. The scoreboard's pictures are each person's public X profile picture, fetched through unavatar.io by their X handle; your own browser does not contact unavatar.io. If you pay for a stamp and send your file on the thank-you page, the file itself is kept for 90 days so the check can run and run again, and is then deleted. Today that door is open only in test mode.

Third-party processors: Cloudflare Pages / Workers (edge execution, L4/L7 termination under Cloudflare privacy standard). Buttondown Inc. (subscription registry under GDPR/CCPA compliance).

Payments: Stripe hosted Checkout, in test mode today; card data never transits this origin. The signature-verified webhook persists the Stripe event, session and payment-intent ids, the SHA-256 work hash, the amount in cents and a timestamp; no name, email or card field is stored.

External resolution: Client-directed requests to MediaWiki APIs (Wikipedia/Wikidata) transmit solely the query term directly from user agent. Grokipedia proxy isolates client IPs, caching entity definitions with 86400s TTL; a per-device counter keyed by the daily salted hash allows 60 lookups a day and expires after 2 days.

Analytics: Cookieless edge beacons (Cloudflare Web Analytics, zero state persistence). First-party daily counter generates SHA-256(DailyRotatingSalt | client address, the /64 network for IPv6) with automatic TTL eviction at 172800s (2 days). No persistent identifier is written to storage.

Bot screening: Cloudflare Turnstile on the Founders form, under Cloudflare's policy. Avatars: the server fetches each board handle's public X picture from unavatar.io; no visitor data is sent. Paid stamp files (/api/stamp-file): the file bytes are stored in KV under their SHA-256 with a 90-day TTL, written only for a paid, unrefunded order; the door answers in test mode only today.

How long we keep it

We keep your email only until you unsubscribe. If you joined the Founders Circle, email hello@humanitieshelpinghands.com anytime to delete your name.

Connector requests and the rate-limit count: not kept; the count lives in memory for about a minute. Newsletter addresses: until you unsubscribe or ask us to delete yours. Founders Circle applications: until you ask us to delete yours; email hello@humanitieshelpinghands.com with your GitHub name and it is removed from storage and from the strength finder. Cloudflare and Buttondown keep their own logs for as long as their own policies say.

Retention bounds: MCP request contexts purge immediately upon stream termination (lifetime < 60s). Newsletter subscriptions persist pending RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe or deletion instruction. Founders Circle KV records persist until owner-initiated tombstone request to hello@humanitieshelpinghands.com.
